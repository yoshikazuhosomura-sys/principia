# Statements, Lean names, and the dependency graph

Companion to the paper, draft v14 (`paper-v14.tex`, 2026-10-02). Three things: (1) the list of statements with grades and Lean names, (2) the dependency DAG as text, (3) the order in which to read the proofs so that nothing is used before it is proved.

The letters (A, B, C, …) are the labels used in the paper for the auxiliary statements. The main theorem has a name, not a letter: **Integrality theorem for the p-adic regulator at split anomalous primes**, short form **Split-integrality theorem**. Section numbers below refer to draft **v14** (2026-10-02): 9 sections + References, §3 has fifteen subsections, §4 thirteen, §5 thirteen; v14 adds Proposition 24ᶜ and Lemma 25 to §3.15 and the canonical threshold to §5.12. The one conditional statement, Proposition V, sits in §6 and in the companion note `note-padic-L-leading-term.md`; it is listed below with the label V and marked **conditional**.

Standing hypotheses unless stated otherwise: E/Q with a global minimal Weierstrass equation, p ≥ 5 of good ordinary reduction, *anomalous* = (p ∣ #E(F_p); for p ≥ 7 this is a_p = 1, i.e. #E(F_p) = p, and at p = 5 it also allows #E(F_5) = 10), *split* = (E(Q_p)[p] ≠ 0), Λ = E(Q)/tors of rank r, Λ₀ = ker(Λ → E(F_p)), ι = (Λ → E(F_p) onto). **Everything from Lemma 4 on assumes #E(F_p) = p.** The height is h_p(P) = 2 log_p(σ_p(P)/d(P)) with σ_p the Mazur–Tate sigma function; in PARI that is −(f − s₂·g), **not** the first component f.

**Two things that depend on the model.** b := v_p(Λ_σ) and c := v_p(A) of Lemma 3 are quantities of a Weierstrass model, not of E: (x, y) ↦ (x, y + sx) with s ∈ Z keeps the model global minimal, fixes x, α, d, φ_p, ψ_p, λ and e, and moves β ↦ β + sαd, so b and c trade while min(b, c) stands. Since p is odd one may take **p ∣ a₁**, and that is the model of Theorem B, Theorem S and Theorem N; there b ≥ min(e + v_p(a₁), 2e), which is 2e for e = 1 and e + v_p(a₁) for e ≥ 2 (Lemma 3′ — the bound b ≥ 2e stated in Lemma 3 is too strong for e ≥ 2, and the difference is exactly what has to be carried at the second digit). The invariant statement behind Theorem S is v_p(h_p(pP′)) = 1.

## 3. Reading order (digesting the proofs)

1. **§2 of the paper** — normalisations. Four traps live here: **which σ** — σ_p is the one fixed by E₂, and PARI's first component belongs to a different one; the factor 2p between the normalisation of [MST] and that of [Har] (a shift of r in the valuation of the regulator); the identity v_p(log_p u) = 1 + v_p(q_p(u)), which makes "second p-adic digit" and "Fermat quotient" interchangeable; and **which model** — b and c are model-dependent, min(b, c) is not.
2. **Lemma 1 → Lemma 2 → Lemma 3** (§4.1). Three short arguments; Lemma 3 is the decomposition that everything after uses. Check here that (A1) and (A2) are both used, and where.
3. **Theorem A** (§4.2). Steps (1)–(2) are the determinant bookkeeping; step (3) is the only place where Hasse's bound and the Tamagawa condition enter.
4. **Lemma 4 and Lemma 4′** (§4.3). Both directions of Lemma 4 are needed. Lemma 4′ is the bridge to the local measurement (splitting is v_p(ψ_p(x₀)) ≥ 2), and it says that only the cut at 2 is lift-independent.
